# Fair Play

Achievements are friendly rewards for real activity. Here is how to enjoy them without causing problems for yourself or others.

## Use one personal account

GitHub's Terms of Service allow one free personal account per person. Some online tutorials tell you to create a second helper account to earn Galaxy Brain or YOLO. Do not do that. Use a real friend or classmate instead, or choose a route that needs no second person, like the practice-repository steps in this repo.

Terms of Service: [GitHub Terms of Service](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service)

## Do not spam maintainers

- No empty or pointless pull requests to other people's projects.
- No opening and instantly closing issues elsewhere.
- No low-effort answers in Discussions.

Every notification lands in a real person's inbox. Practice in your own repository, and contribute to others only when you have something useful to add.

## Do not buy or fake stars

Purchased or bot-driven stars count as rank abuse under GitHub's Acceptable Use Policies, and they do nothing for the quality of your project.

## Credit honestly

Only add a `Co-authored-by` line for people who actually worked on the commit, and only with their agreement.

## Keep perspective

A profile with three honest pull requests and a project people use says more about you than a wall of badges. Treat badges as a checklist for learning the workflow: branches, pull requests, reviews, issues, discussions and sponsorship. That workflow is the skill.
